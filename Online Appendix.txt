*Competition and persistence. Within the non-financial economy, surplus-ratio deviations are 8–9% closed each year; across the finance boundary the differential does not close in the full sample, and the full-sample figure is concentrated in the 2022–24 FISIM repricing, so the coefficient cannot separate an entry barrier from the rate cycle. The candidate obstacles are concrete where they can be checked, banking and insurance licences, the conduit position and land-supply control; risk premia and the FISIM premium are rival readings. The unweighted dispersion of industry surplus ratios rose from 0.45 to 0.73 (2004–2023), the output-weighted measure only to 0.53: non-financial competition functions; what it cannot do is cross the boundary. The persistence coefficients are consistent with an entry barrier but cannot separate it from risk, composition or measurement (Table B1 for the levels).*

*Industry-level price–surplus-ratio correlations: price change against surplus ratio change, +0.53 over 2007–23 and +0.65 over 2019–23 (seven industries with matched deflator and surplus ratio series; excluding finance, +0.37 and +0.62; rank correlations +0.54 and +0.50). Financial-intermediation content of the consumption price, direct and indirect through the Leontief inverse: 8.1% (2007), 4.7% (2014), 5.5% (2016), 9.3% (2018), 10.6% (2019), 15.5% (2023), 15.8% (2024). The ADB household-consumption vector changes in 2017–18, its import share falling from 36 to 18%, so the series is read within segments: within the later segment the rise runs from 9.3 to 15.8, four-fifths of it after 2019, and the level includes financial-industry surplus and is not an isolated estimate or causal upper bound of FISIM repricing. The financial surplus coefficient applies the C&SD financial-industry GOS/(GOS + employee compensation) ratio to the ADB finance value-added coefficient; direct refers to final finance demand, and indirect includes all intermediate requirements including self-purchases. Within the earlier segment the content fell from 8.1 to 5.5, which is adverse to any reading of downward stickiness unless the deposit side of FISIM explains it. Manufacturing's surplus ratio fell from about 10% (2004) to 2.3% (2023), the decline complete before the pandemic; accommodation and food, at −0.5% in 2023, combines a long squeeze with the pandemic's aftermath.*

Borrowing-first substitutes Δln(1+b) for the borrowing term and Δln h−Δln(1+b) for interest. These ordered allocations are not causal effects.*

**Volume sensitivity.** Replace g by real private machinery and IP investment divided by Π<sub>B</sub> deflated with the GDP deflator; retain nominal q and b. For 2012–14 to 2017–19 the interest / borrowing / conversion shares are 24.8 / 64.3 / 10.9% (HIBOR), 32.5 / 65.0 / 2.5% (HIBOR + 1.5), and 24.8 / 70.7 / 4.5% (prime). For 2017–19 to 2022–24 they are 18.0 / 43.7 / 38.3%, 18.9 / 45.8 / 35.3%, and 4.3 / 48.7 / 47.0%, respectively. The result is a purchasing-power sensitivity, not an additive cash-flow account; the GDP deflator is not an industry deflator. Chained-volume components are not additive. Nominal and volume allocations differ materially.

**Demand and 2025 checks.** BIS broad REER annual average (2020=100) rose from 86.74 in 2014 to 97.84 in 2016 (+12.8%). Correlations of annual log changes in REER with log changes in real private equipment and IP investment at k=−2, −1, 0, +1, +2 years are +0.24, +0.03, −0.43, −0.02 and −0.08 (n=17, 18, 19, 18, 17); positive k means REER leads. These are exploratory, not causal. In 2025 the linked mapped loan stock rose from HK$1,871.154bn to HK$1,880.252bn (+0.49%), while private equipment and IP investment volume rose 34.6%. A substantial rise in investment coincided with a small change in mapped lending, without identifying the source of finance. Sources: BIS/FRED RBHKBIS; C&SD 310-32032; HKMA C03.

## B18. Transport fixed-asset shift-share

The ASEA transport survey covers net additions to fixed assets, including machinery, equipment and buildings. Air transport is excluded in the second comparison to reduce the influence of large, lumpy aircraft purchases. For each window, industry fixed-asset additions and gross surplus are summed over the three years. Let rᵢ denote additions divided by surplus and wᵢ the industry's share of the sample's surplus. The symmetric within component is Σ[(w₀+w₁)/2](r₁−r₀), and the composition component is Σ[(r₀+r₁)/2](w₁−w₀). The components sum to the change in the sample ratio.

| Transport sample | 2012–14 | 2017–19 | Change, pp | Within, pp | Composition, pp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All ten sub-industries | 72.09% | 71.76% | −0.33 | −2.51 | +2.18 |
| Excluding air transport and auxiliary activities | 32.16% | 29.51% | −2.65 | +1.01 | −3.66 |

The ex-air sample is renormalised over the remaining nine sub-industries; their aggregate annual average net additions were HK$10.95bn and HK$10.67bn. The survey's all-fixed-asset measure and the national accounts' private machinery-and-IP investment differ in coverage and valuation. The ASEA measure includes all fixed assets, including land, buildings, equipment and disposals; national-accounts private machinery and IP formation has different institutional, asset and valuation boundaries. The difference in their two window changes (HK$0.283bn versus HK$57.661bn, or an arithmetic 0.49%) is a cross-measure scale comparison, not a contribution or attributable share. The nine-industry sample includes land passenger transport. The within component is an industry-level weighted decomposition and does not establish that every continuing firm maintained investment.
